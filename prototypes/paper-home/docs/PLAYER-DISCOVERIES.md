# Data-driven player discovery

Players listing, below season leaders: two compact tabs, Season outliers / Players to watch (Kauden yllättäjät / Pidä silmällä). Desktop four cards; mobile horizontal swipe rail. All data follows the selected league and season. Profile links preserve that scope. English copy is included.

Outliers compare the same player with the immediately previous available season: assists and rebounds per 40 min, eFG%, minutes per game. IDs are preferred; an exact normalized full name is used only when unique in both cohorts. Current minimum: max(2, ceil(max player games / 3)) games and 15 minutes per required appearance. Previous sample: at least 8 games and 120 minutes. eFG needs at least 30 current and 80 previous field-goal attempts. Change thresholds: 1.5 AST/40, 2.5 REB/40, 7 eFG percentage points (UI uses %), 5 minutes/game. Candidates are ordered by absolute change relative to that threshold and a capped square-root minutes factor. Different players and both directions are shown where qualifying data exists. Playing-time changes use a neutral role label; team changes and both samples are visible. These are descriptive screens, not significance tests or causal estimates.

Watchlist selects from the top 15% of qualified peers in assists/40, rebounds/40, steals/40 and eFG, one player per skill without repeats. Additional minimum totals: 6 assists, 10 rebounds, 3 steals or 30 field-goal attempts. Peer means pool minutes or attempts; scoring totals are not used for selection. A capped square-root minutes factor prefers a larger sample among high-ranked candidates. Ranking and peer count are shown. Qualification grows during the season. Early seasons (under 8 player appearances) have an explicit early-signal label.

No ages, rookie labels or career-history assumptions are inferred. The user accepted these two sections first; Rising stars requires verified birthdates and career coverage later. Counts and strengths do not claim overall player quality, defensive impact or future success. Tests cover time-normalization, sparse shooting samples, ambiguous identities, point-independent picks, weighted averages, unique picks, both change directions and sample qualification.
